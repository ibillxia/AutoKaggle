**[Open Pool v11](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/crtproduction/name-that-llm-open-pool)** has **190,588 passages**. It is slightly smaller than v10 on purpose: the labels are more trustworthy, and you get new tools for choosing data. The image below is the short version.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-forum-message-attachments/o/inbox%2F20960131%2F1e8999e997ac6b3f11ae5ad0d168970d%2Ffig5_v11_overview.png?generation=1793566058704601&alt=media)
### 1. What I tested

Same setup as before: a small Qwen3-1.7B LoRA, 45,000 passages, trained on pool data only and scored on the 2,699 real train passages. Each idea changed one thing against v10 (minus one source that is no longer public).

Numbers are the change in balanced accuracy, using the mean logits of 2 seeds. The range is a 95% interval from resampling prompts.

| Change tested alone | All 150 train prompts | Seed 0 / seed 1 | 2 answers per prompt | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.7 from Vertex AI sources only | +0.009 (0.000 to +0.019) | +0.007 / +0.006 | +0.009 (-0.016 to +0.034) | **Adopted** |
| Sampling matched to real typography and length | +0.026 (+0.012 to +0.039) | +0.013 / +0.022 | +0.012 (-0.021 to +0.045) | **Added as a column** |
| Prompts with the most labels first | +0.009 (-0.005 to +0.022) | +0.016 / -0.003 | -0.006 (-0.036 to +0.027) | Not proven |
| Adding prvsiyan's extended rows | -0.009 (-0.023 to +0.004) | -0.001 / -0.005 | -0.004 (-0.035 to +0.026) | Not added |

**How strong is this?** The first two changes point the right way on every cut and on both seeds. But on the 2-answer cut, which is closest to the test shape, the interval still includes zero. So treat them as likely gains, not proven ones. I tested each change alone: the final v11 file as a whole and its new passages were not part of these tests.

### 2. gemini-3.7-flash now means Vertex AI

Competitors documented that since 2026-10-08, other APIs answer "gemini-3.7" requests with 3.8. The data agrees: inside one source of 7.5k rows, a classifier could not separate its 3.7 rows from its 3.8 rows (AUC 0.498), while 3.6 vs 3.8 scored 0.776.

- **The main file now keeps only 3,278 rows with a documented Vertex AI route** for this label.
- **The other 10,593 rows moved to `gemini37_unverified.parquet`.** Nothing is deleted, so you can make your own call.
- **Recall on real passages:** 3.7 went from 0.21 to 0.42, and 3.8 from 0.59 to 0.64. **3.6 fell from 0.63 to 0.47**, so this is not a free win for every label. The total goes up.

If you generated 3.7 through Vertex AI and it is in the unverified file, tell me where this is documented and I will move those rows back.

### 3. New: `match_weight`

The pool's typography is still different from real passages. For example, word hyphens appear in 44% of the pool's Gemini passages but only 12% of real ones. The new column reweights passages inside each label so these rates match.

```python
import pandas as pd
pool = pd.read_parquet("/kaggle/input/name-that-llm-open-pool/ntl_open_pool.parquet")

# train on the whole file, or sample a fixed number per label matched to real typography
sample = pool.groupby("model", group_keys=False).apply(
    lambda d: d.sample(min(2500, len(d)), weights="match_weight", random_state=0))
```

The weights use all train prompts, so a validation on train.csv after using them will read slightly high. My test fitted on one half of the prompts and scored the other half (+0.027 on that half).

### 4. New: recovered prompts, thanks to the comment on prompt coverage

A commenter on this thread suspected that many prompts have answers from only a subset of the 18 LLMs. They were right.

`prompt_group` turned out to be unreliable: one group could be a whole file, and the same prompt in two sources became two groups. So I recovered the real prompt for each passage and matched prompts across sources.

- **`prompt_canon`** gives you a prompt id for 99% of rows, for cleaner group splits.
- **`prompts.parquet`** has the text of 13,021 prompts.
- **592 prompts are now answered by all 18 labels** (and I can see exactly which are not).
- **`panel_gaps.csv`** lists 4,472 prompts with 10 to 17 labels and which labels they miss. **If you want to help the community, generate these.** The most-missing labels are gemini-3.7-flash, qwen3.8-max, kimi-k3 and claude-fable-5.

"Prompts with the most labels first" did not repeat its gain on the second seed, so I am not recommending it yet. `panel_labels` lets you test it yourself. Your result may well differ with a bigger model.

### 5. A warning about one metric

My earlier test tables include a "prompt-centred" column, which subtracts each prompt's mean logits. With 17 to 18 answers per prompt, it over-predicts some labels: my baseline predicted gemini-3.1 466 times at 0.32 precision, and never predicted kimi-k3. So I did not use it for these decisions. I added the 2-answers-per-prompt cut instead, since that is closer to the test. If you use this kind of score on full prompts, check your prediction counts per label.

### Things to know before you train on it

- **Not tested on the LB yet.** The new passages went through the same filters as v11, but I have not trained a model on v12.
- **The new Claude passages answer synthetic prompts, and their typography is off.** The em dash appears in 55% of the new `claude-fable-5` rows against 83% in real passages. `match_weight` corrects each label's rate. - **Claude now holds a bigger share of the file.** Balance classes when you sample.
- **The kilupy licence comes from the README.** The datasets' Kaggle licence field is unset, but their READMEs state CC BY 4.0, and `source_license` says so.
- **`prompt_canon` changed on 482 v11 rows.** The new sources added prompt evidence. All other v11 columns are unchanged.

Thanks for the controlled comparison and for calling +0.002 noise. One thing that may help with reading such pairs:
the noise of a *difference* between two submissions is not the noise of one score. Both are scored on the same public
rows, so only the rows where exactly one of the two is right move the gap. If f is that share, the sd of the public
gap is about sqrt(f / n_public), which for ~1,200 public rows is:

| f (rows where exactly one is right) | 1% | 2% | 5% | 10% | 20% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sd of the public gap | 0.003 | 0.004 | 0.006 | 0.009 | 0.013 |

You can measure f on your OOF before submitting: compare the two models' correctness row by row.

- Two separately trained models (different pools, or even the same code with another seed) can easily disagree in
  correctness on 10-20% of rows. That gives an sd of 0.009-0.013, the size of the ~0.009 that alexchilton measured,
  and +0.002 says nothing either way, as you wrote.
- Two blends that differ in one small weight are a different story. My 0.621 and 0.623 blends disagree in
  correctness on only 1% of OOF rows, so their gap has an sd of about 0.003. There, 0.002 is still under one sd, but
  a 0.01 gap would be over three sd, i.e. real.

I checked the formula by simulation (balanced accuracy, 18 classes, random 30% public split; it matched within ±6%
everywhere) and put it in a CPU notebook, with a helper that estimates f from your own OOF predictions:
https://www.kaggle.com/code/locdunhat/is-a-0-002-lb-gain-real

Caveat: it assumes independent rows. Real errors cluster by prompt and topic, so treat these numbers as a floor.
