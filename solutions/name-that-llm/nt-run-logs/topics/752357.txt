## TL;DR

We sit at 0.639 (an unmodified public notebook, credited in ours). We have **no scored change of our own in this competition that beat its parent**: our stylegram addition scored 0.550 and a GLM gate 0.555. So this post is about what we learned from reading the public lineage and from the parts we built and tested ourselves.

## What the public lineage shows (55 notebooks, 134 scored versions)

| Change type | What it bought | Repeats? |
|---|---|---|
| Bigger / cleaner training pool (35k → 84k) | +0.028, then +0.003, +0.002 | yes, the biggest lever |
| Two-stage recipe (public pool first, then train) | +0.023 once | one-off |

| New large LLM members | early +0.005 / +0.006; nothing above 0.61 | no |
| Reweighting, typography guards, Hungarian assignment | flat to negative | no |

The encoders hurt the 0.636 (0.634) most likely because desklib, UltraBERT, ELECTRA and DeBERTa are already inside its complementary stack, so they were counted twice.

## What we built and tested ourselves

1. **The stylegram addition and a GLM gate** (earlier): 0.550 and 0.555. Our simulation scored the stylegram alone; inside the blend it doubled the weight of evidence already present.
2. **A rebuild of the two encoder members** (ModernBERT-large, desklib): out-of-fold 0.553 and 0.499 against nuridudak's 0.569 and 0.523, because we used a 75k pool against his 122k. We did not submit it, since we expected about 0.622-0.625.
3. **A re-implementation of ATLAS** (shared LoRA with per-source up-projections on a frozen Qwen3-8B): verified to load in the public notebook and match its output, not trained at full scale.

A practical note for anyone copying notebooks: if the original runs on the "latest" environment, don't pin its image digest. We pinned `kaggle-images/python@sha256:17b2fc…`, which is Kaggle's CPU image, and got "Torch not compiled with CUDA" despite a GPU being enabled.

## Open questions

- **Licences and rule 2.6.** The 0.639's readout bank is fit partly on 4,404 privately generated passages and its dataset licence is "unknown"; the train-only version is 0.637. Has the host answered adisak's question about privately generated data?
- Has anyone put Gaussian group heads on the two 14B LoRAs of the 0.620 main? We can't find it in any public version, and it repeated about +0.005 four times.

## Would we reach gold?

Gold sits at about 0.653 and the top at 0.700. We think the gap from 0.639 is **mostly data, not architecture**: the #1 poster writes that part of their pre-external-data 0.583 may have been probing, and #3 reports that removing an API passage set dropped validation 0.463 → 0.456. The one concrete idea we plan to test is Gaussian heads on the 14B LoRAs, which would still leave us short. Our odds of gold are **low**.
