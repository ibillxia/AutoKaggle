# Figure captions

## Figure 1: What the cohort can and cannot support

`figures/fig01_data_coverage.png`

**Takeaway.** Every focus position group has enough prospects for group-level inference, but the effective N is tens per group, not hundreds -- so any claim must be position-stratified and uncertainty must be shown.

## Figure 2: The sensor layer reproduces the stopwatch it is meant to augment

`figures/fig02_sensor_validity.png`

**Takeaway.** Sensor-derived drill durations track the official electronic times closely, which is the precondition for trusting any sensor-derived movement trait. Note the residual spread: that scatter is exactly the information the stopwatch throws away.

## Figure 3: Two hazards that would silently bias a naive analysis

`figures/fig03_hazards.png`

**Takeaway.** A quarter to a third of prospects skip at least one agility test, and the 2023 draft class has roughly three times the NFL exposure of the 2025 class. Complete-case analysis and raw career totals are therefore both traps.

## Figure 4: The screen, not the narrative, chooses the topic

`figures/fig04_feasibility_map.png`

**Takeaway.** Across every pre-registered sensor-trait x in-game-outcome pair, the large majority fail to add out-of-sample explanatory power beyond the conventional Combine battery and draft capital. The surviving pairs are concentrated in a few drill families, which is what makes the eventual topic selection credible rather than cherry-picked.

## Figure 5: The headline result, with its uncertainty

`figures/fig05_main_linkage.png`

**Takeaway.** After residualising both the sensor trait and the NFL outcome on the entire conventional Combine battery plus draft capital, the association remains. The right panel is the honest version of the claim: an interval, not a point.

## Figure 6: Sensor tracking adds information the stopwatch cannot express

`figures/fig06_incremental_value.png`

**Takeaway.** The sensor metric and the full conventional battery are not substitutes: the gain is in the combination. Read honestly, this chart also shows when the metric does NOT beat the best single stopwatch test -- which is reported rather than hidden.

## Figure 7: The actionable output: named disagreements between the two evaluation systems

`figures/fig07_blind_spots.png`

**Takeaway.** Players in the upper-left quadrant move like the sensor's idea of a good prospect while the stopwatch says otherwise, and their in-game outcome is what arbitrates. This is the list a front office can actually use in the week before the draft.

## Figure 8: The metric as a decision tool, and how much to trust the ranking

`figures/fig08_metric_leaderboard.png`

**Takeaway.** The left panel is what a scout would read; the right panel exists to stop them over-reading it. A metric whose top-10 barely survives resampling should be used to screen, not to rank.

## Figure 9: Adversarial checks on the headline claim

`figures/fig09_robustness.png`

**Takeaway.** If removing any single prospect collapses or reverses the effect, the finding is a story about one player rather than a property of the position group. Trait reliability bounds how strong any correlation could possibly have been.
