--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. BACKGROUND AND PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every spring, college football's top prospects gather in Indianapolis for the NFL
Scouting Combine. For decades, player evaluation at the Combine has relied on
standardized stopwatch timings and physical testing numbers, such as the 40-yard
dash, short shuttle, 3-cone drill, and vertical jump. While these numbers provide
a baseline measure of straight-line speed and explosiveness, player tracking
sensors worn during workouts now capture the full detail of HOW athletes move
throughout each drill.

The 2027 Big Data Bowl provides player tracking data from the NFL Scouting
Combine alongside regular-season NFL game tracking data. The goal is to better
understand how player movement during Combine drills translates to performance
on the field during the NFL regular season.

Participants must build an analytical framework that uncovers non-obvious,
actionable linkages between 10 Hz Combine sensor tracking data and future
regular-season NFL game performance. The submission should look beyond
conventional scouting wisdom and identify novel signals that help coaches and
evaluators identify hidden talent, avoid costly draft busts, and design
position-specific player development protocols.

Participants are encouraged to focus on ONE small aspect of movement, a specific
position group, or an individual drill, rather than attempting to evaluate every
prospect across all drills.

Examples suggested by the organizers (non-exhaustive):
  * Route running and coverage -- measuring change of direction, deceleration
    into breaks, and acceleration out of cuts for receivers and defensive backs,
    and comparing to in-game separation.
  * Trench play -- evaluating first-step quickness, lateral agility, and burst
    for offensive and defensive linemen during agility drills and comparing to
    pass-rush or run-blocking performance.
  * Drill translation -- comparing sensor-based movement metrics with traditional
    drill times to identify where tracking data adds context to prospect
    evaluation.
  * Position-specific mechanics -- analyzing body control, acceleration profiles,
    or pursuit angles during position drills and their relationship to NFL rookie
    performance.

3. CRITICAL STRUCTURAL FACT ABOUT THIS COMPETITION (READ CAREFULLY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a HACKATHON, not a predictive-modeling leaderboard competition.

  * There is NO train.csv / test.csv split.
  * There is NO evaluation metric (no RMSE / AUC / logloss).
  * There is NO leaderboard to optimize against.
  * There is NO sample_submission.csv in the usual sense.

The "submission" is a WRITEUP. Therefore the AutoKaggle tabular pipeline
(train -> clean -> feature engineer -> fit model -> predict -> submit) does NOT
apply directly and MUST be re-targeted to an insight-generation pipeline:
  hypothesis search -> statistical validation -> metric construction ->
  communication (figures + writeup).

physical testing results, and multi-season NFL game performance metrics for 510

partitioned into 9 CSV files linking pre-draft physical traits directly to

8. WHY THIS COMPETITION IS HARD (modelling / validity hazards)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * N is tiny at the player level (510 prospects; a single position group can be
    50-120 players). Effect sizes must be interpreted with wide uncertainty.
  * Heavy MULTIPLE-COMPARISONS risk: hundreds of candidate kinematic features x
    dozens of outcomes. Naive "best correlation" findings are almost certainly
    false positives. Any credible analysis needs either pre-registered
    hypotheses, cross-validated incremental validity, or explicit FDR control.
  * CONFOUNDING BY DRAFT CAPITAL / OPPORTUNITY: higher draft picks receive more
    snaps, more targets and more pass-rush reps. A raw correlation between a
    Combine trait and an in-game counting stat may only reflect draft position.
    Outcome models must control for draft capital, or use rate/opportunity-
    normalized outcomes.
  * POSITION HETEROGENEITY: the same drill means different things for a 320 lb
    defensive tackle and a 180 lb corner. Analyses must be position-stratified or
    position-adjusted; pooling all 510 players will wash out real signal.
  * SURVIVORSHIP: career-cumulative outcomes (career snaps, All-Pro) are only
    observed for players who stayed in the league; 2025 draftees have far less
    exposure than 2023 draftees. Season exposure must be normalized.
  * TRACKING ARTIFACTS: 10 Hz optical tracking is noisy; raw `a` is a finite
    difference and is extremely noisy. Kinematic features should be smoothed
    before differentiating (e.g. Savitzky-Golay) and should be cross-validated
    against the official electronic timings in combine_results.csv.
  * DRILL MIXTURE: drill_type / drill_name are unbalanced across positions and
    years; attempts per player vary. Features must be defined per attempt and
    aggregated carefully (best attempt vs mean attempt).
  * The competition explicitly asks for ACTIONABLE and NOVEL insights -- a
    marginally significant regression coefficient is not a deliverable. The
    output must be a metric, a ranking, or a decision rule that a coach, scout or
    front office could actually use on a week-to-week basis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. WHAT A WINNING SUBMISSION LOOKS LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * One crisp, well-motivated question, not a survey of everything.
  * A sensor-derived movement metric that is NOT already captured by the
    stopwatch numbers in combine_results.csv (this is the entire point: "where
    tracking data adds context to prospect evaluation").
  * A demonstrated link to a concrete in-game outcome (separation, get-off,
    pressure rate, coverage, snaps) with correct statistics and stated
    uncertainty.
  * Explicit handling of the confounds above (draft capital, position, exposure).
  * A validation that the new metric beats / adds to the traditional Combine
    numbers -- quantified as incremental explanatory power.
  * 3-5 excellent figures that a non-technical football audience can read.
  * A reproducible public notebook.
